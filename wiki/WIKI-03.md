# WIKI-03: Spaced Repetition & Optimal Intervals

[← Back to Wiki Index](./README.md)

## Core Concept
**Spaced repetition** spreads study sessions out over gradually expanding time gaps, timing each review right before a memory is about to fade.

## Mechanism (How It Works)
Each timely retrieval flattens the forgetting curve—making the subsequent decay slope significantly slower—and triggers cellular maintenance of long-term memory traces without overloading working memory.

## Practical Takeaway
Follow an expanding review schedule instead of a single long session:
1. **Day 0:** Initial learning and encoding
2. **Day 1:** First active-recall review (interrupts steepest drop)
3. **Day 3–7:** Second review
4. **Day 14–16:** Third review
5. **Day 30+:** Long-term maintenance check

*(Adaptive rule: shorten the gap for items you miss; widen the gap for items you recall easily.)*

## Common Myth / Trap
> **Myth:** *"Studying 5 hours in one Sunday session equals 1 hour spread across 5 days."*  
> **Reality:** Massed practice (cramming) spikes short-term familiarity for tomorrow morning, but retention collapses rapidly compared to distributed spacing.
