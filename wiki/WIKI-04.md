# WIKI-04: Desirable Difficulties & Interleaving

[← Back to Wiki Index](./README.md)

## Core Concept
Coined by cognitive psychologists Robert and Elizabeth Bjork, **desirable difficulties** are learning conditions that feel slower and more effortful during practice—such as spacing, self-testing, varying study environments, and **interleaving**—yet produce substantially stronger long-term retention and transfer.

## Mechanism (How It Works)
**Blocked practice** (drilling `AAAA → BBBB → CCCC`) allows learners to run on autopilot without deciding *which* concept applies. **Interleaving** (mixing `A → B → C → B → A`) forces the brain to continually discriminate between problem types, mirroring real exam and real-world conditions.

## Practical Takeaway
* Mix different problem types or related topics within a single study session instead of drilling one type 20 times in a row.
* Occasionally vary where and how you study rather than relying on one narrow environmental cue.

## Common Myth / Trap
> **Myth:** *"If studying feels effortless and error-free, I'm learning fast."*  
> **Reality:** Bjork & Bjork emphasize that **current performance** during practice is a poor predictor of **long-term learning**. Productive struggle during practice is a sign of deeper encoding.
