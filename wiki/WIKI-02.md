# WIKI-02: Active Recall & The Testing Effect

[← Back to Wiki Index](./README.md)

## Core Concept
Active recall (also called retrieval practice or the testing effect) is the habit of deliberately pulling information *out* of your memory without looking at notes, rather than passively re-reading or highlighting.

## Mechanism (How It Works)
Effortful retrieval strengthens neural pathways between the hippocampus and neocortex, signaling to the brain that the memory trace is worth stabilizing. In classic cognitive psychology experiments (e.g., Roediger & Karpicke), retrieval practice yielded **~80% retention** after one week compared to **~34%** for passive restudy.

## Practical Takeaway
* Close your notebook and summarize everything you remember on a blank sheet (**"blurting"**).
* Turn section headings into self-test questions instead of reading bullet points.
* Spend follow-up study time specifically on the gaps you failed to recall.

## Common Myth / Trap
> Myth: "Re-reading feels smooth and familiar, so I know the material."
> Reality: Re-reading creates an **illusion of fluency**—recognizing familiar words feels like mastery, even when you cannot reconstruct the answer on a blank exam page.
