# WIKI-06: Sleep & Memory Consolidation

[← Back to Wiki Index](./README.md)

## Core Concept
Memory formation operates across three stages—**Acquisition** (taking information in), **Consolidation** (stabilizing it), and **Recall** (accessing it later)—and sleep is when the brain actively executes consolidation.

## Mechanism (How It Works)
During sleep, the brain replays daytime neural activity and strengthens key synaptic connections:
* **Slow-Wave (Deep) Sleep:** Stabilizes factual and conceptual (**declarative**) memories learned earlier that day.
* **REM Sleep:** Integrates skill-based (**procedural**) learning, emotional context, and creative connections across ideas.

The night **immediately after** learning is an especially critical consolidation window.

## Practical Takeaway
Protect **7–9 hours of sleep** on nights following study sessions, and pair daytime spaced study with normal overnight sleep rather than late-night cramming.

## Common Myth / Trap
> **Myth:** *"Pulling an all-nighter buys me extra study hours."*  
> **Reality:** Skipping sleep sacrifices the exact biological process required to save what you studied—and later "catch-up" sleep cannot fully recover lost consolidation.
